Introduction

This guide is applicable to the following device / devices:
TMU CM4
CAN-FD Pro
CAN-FD Pro +IP67 casing

AutoPi power management controls when the device is awake, sleeping, or hibernating. These states allow the device to collect and upload data while protecting the vehicle's battery.

This guide explains each power state, what causes the device to change states, and how the status LEDs indicate its current state. To configure when the device sleeps, see Sleep Timers.

tip

For device-specific power cycle, LED, and button behavior, see LED and Button on AutoPi TMU CM4 Devices or LED and Button on AutoPi CAN-FD Pro Devices.

How Power Management Works​

Power management has two layers:

  • Low-level power management controls the hardware states and wake triggers through the Smart Power Management (SPM) system. To learn more about the underlying service, see SPM Manager.
  • High-level power management uses AutoPi Core commands, settings, and events to decide when the device should sleep or hibernate.

Power Cycle​

The device uses three main operating states:

StateDescriptionHow the Device Leaves the State
OnThe device collects data, runs configured tasks, and communicates with AutoPi Cloud.A sleep timer expires, or a hibernation threshold is reached.
SleepingThe Raspberry Pi is powered off for a configured interval to reduce power consumption.A wake timer expires, or a configured source such as a voltage change, the modem, or the accelerometer triggers a wake-up.
HibernatingAlmost all components are powered off to protect a low vehicle battery.The STN chip detects that the voltage has risen above the configured wake threshold.

When the Vehicle Is Parked​

When you connect the device while the vehicle's engine is off, the typical cycle is:

  1. The device boots and connects to AutoPi Cloud.
  2. The device creates a sleep timer. The default timer is five minutes, but the configured value may differ.
  3. When the timer expires, the device sets a hardware wake timer and attempts to upload pending data.
  4. The device shuts down and enters the Sleeping state.
  5. When the wake interval expires, the device boots again. If the vehicle is still inactive, the device creates another sleep timer and repeats the cycle.

This periodic wake-up allows the device to process tasks and upload data without remaining on continuously. See Sleep Timers to configure the awake period and sleep interval.

When the Vehicle Starts​

If the device is sleeping when the engine of a standard internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicle starts, the typical sequence is:

  1. The STN chip detects that the vehicle's battery voltage has risen above the configured wake threshold.
  2. The STN chip sends a wake signal, and the device boots.
  3. The device detects CAN bus traffic and RPM data.
  4. The RPM data triggers the vehicle/engine/running event.
  5. The device clears its sleep timers and prevents new timers from being set while the vehicle is running.

If the device is already on when the vehicle starts, it remains on and clears its sleep timers without rebooting.

caution

Vehicles that do not provide standard OBD-II engine data may require additional configuration to keep the device awake while driving. For electric vehicles, see Configure the Power Cycle for Electric Vehicles.

When the Battery Voltage Is Low​

Hibernation protects the vehicle's battery when its voltage falls too low. You can configure two hibernation thresholds under Devices > select a device > Advanced Settings > Power:

SettingBehavior
Critical LevelStarts a graceful shutdown. Before entering hibernation, the device attempts to upload pending data and report the event to AutoPi Cloud.
Safety Cut-OutImmediately cuts power to prevent further battery drain. AutoPi Cloud receives a notification after the device comes back online.

The device can report a critical-level shutdown to AutoPi Cloud only when it has an internet connection. While the battery voltage remains above both thresholds, the device continues its configured wake-and-sleep cycle.

Status LEDs​

The status LEDs provide a quick visual indication of the device's current power state and connection to AutoPi Cloud.

note

This guide and the LED states below apply only to AutoPi TMU CM4/CAN-FD Pro devices. For information about the LED indicators on AutoPi Mini devices, see LED Lights of an AutoPi Mini Device.

Blue LED​

The blue LED shows the state of the low-level SPM system.

LED StateDescription
OffThe SPM system is off.
Bright flashingThe SPM system is booting.
Constant bright lightThe SPM system is on.
Breathing effectThe SPM system is sleeping.
Constant dim lightThe SPM system is hibernating.

Green LED​

The green LED shows the state of the high-level power management system and its connection to AutoPi Cloud.

LED StateDescription
OffThe system is not running or is still booting.
Bright flashingThe system is running but is not connected to AutoPi Cloud.
Constant bright lightThe system is running and connected to AutoPi Cloud.
note

For LED light indicators on the AutoPi Mini device, see LED Lights of an AutoPi Mini Device.
